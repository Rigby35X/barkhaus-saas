Rescue directors didn't get into animal rescue to spend six hours a week on spreadsheets. Yet here we are.

Here are the five admin tasks that eat the most time at small rescue organizations — and how to reclaim your calendar.

## 1. Manually updating the website

The typical rescue workflow: someone updates an animal's status in a Google Sheet, then separately logs into a website builder, finds the animal's page, updates the status, and re-publishes.

That's four steps for one change. Multiply by 20 animals a month and you've lost a full workday.

**The fix:** Your animal records and your website should be the same thing. When you update a status in your database, the website updates automatically. That's how Barkhaus works.

## 2. Managing applications via email

Adoption applications submitted through PDF forms or generic email addresses create chaos. Who's reviewing which application? Did anyone respond to that one from two weeks ago?

**The fix:** A unified inbox that assigns applications to reviewers and tracks status. Simple, auditable, no more falling through the cracks.

## 3. Writing every social post from scratch

The average rescue posts 3-5 times per week. At 15 minutes per post, that's over an hour a week on content creation alone — for a volunteer-run organization.

**The fix:** AI content generation gets you 80% of the way there in 30 seconds. You add the personality. Total time: 2-3 minutes per post.

## 4. Sending individual follow-up emails

"Hi, just wanted to follow up on your application for Biscuit…" Typed out. One by one. For every application.

**The fix:** Template replies with a mail merge field for the animal name. One click, personalized, done.

## 5. Generating documents from scratch

Adoption agreements, foster contracts, volunteer waivers — every rescue needs them, and most are generating them on request from a Word doc template.

**The fix:** Standardized, pre-approved templates that auto-fill with adopter information. Review once, deploy forever.
